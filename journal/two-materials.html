When Two Materials Meet

April 2026 · Berlin

This essay closes the washi tape / clear plastic sequence by describing what happens on the rare occasions both materials appear in a single painting. The tape goes in first, waiting hidden beneath weeks of subsequent layers, until at some point it's peeled away to expose whatever surface sat underneath from the very start.

What follows changes the meaning of the clear plastic layer entirely: instead of performing transparency over a finished, settled painting, it now covers a moment that has just become vulnerable — the freshly revealed surface underneath the peeled tape. In dialogue with Claude, OBOSHI likens the full sequence to a breath: the tape as the patient inhale, the peeling as the pause between, and the plastic as the exhale that gently covers what was just opened.

He notes that sometimes the tape is never peeled at all, and the plastic arrives anyway — covering a secret it doesn't even know is there, two layers keeping the same silence for entirely different reasons. The essay frames the result as something with no name yet: a lightness or rightness that appears only when the sequence works, produced by the materials' own logic rather than anything fully planned in advance.
