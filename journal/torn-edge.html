Essays from the Studio

The Torn Edge

May 2026 · Berlin

OBOSHI treats a tear, scratch, or frayed edge in a painting not as an accident but as an opening — the moment a canvas stops behaving like a container and starts behaving like a membrane under pressure. When something ruptures, color and silence redistribute, and the work shifts, in his framing, from an object into an event: a first vibration originating in the material itself.

A second vibration comes from inside the author — a quiet tremor beneath every decision, made of freedom colliding with time, hesitation overlapping with urgency, running right up until the piece leaves the painter's hands. A third vibration only appears once the work reaches a viewer, who isn't simply looking at the painting but seeing themselves through it; the work's rhythm shifts to meet the viewer's own inner space, reaching places the material itself never anticipated.

Together these three vibrations don't line up end to end, OBOSHI argues, but form a circulating field — material to author to viewer and back again in new form. The essay's closing claim is that a painting is actually completed inside its viewer, and takes as many finished forms as there are viewers standing in front of it. A short dialogue with Claude echoes the earlier idea that timing belongs to the material rather than the author, closing on the sense of a third presence hovering between painter and material that opens directly into the questions of silence and inevitability that follow.

This piece is part of an ongoing numbered essay series, written with AI as co-author. The full essay continues on Medium.
