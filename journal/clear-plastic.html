Painted Conversations

What Clear Plastic Sees

April 2026 · Berlin

Once a canvas feels finished, OBOSHI describes starting an entirely separate painting on a sheet of clear plastic — paint, collage, sometimes more washi — before laying it over the original. The effect, worked through in dialogue with Claude, is that a viewer is always looking at two paintings at once without any way to tell where one ends and the other begins.

The material becomes a study in a specific kind of contradiction: it appears to reveal everything, since it's transparent, while simultaneously adding its own world on top and controlling exactly what gets seen underneath. The dialogue lands on a comparison to something very human — a surface that performs openness while quietly protecting whatever it doesn't want examined too closely.

Some finished works use only this plastic layer, with no hidden washi tape beneath — pieces the essay describes as exposed and guarded at the same time, the way a person might speak freely about everything except the one thing that matters most. The piece ends by setting up what happens when the plastic and the tape appear together in the same painting — a question the following essay takes up directly.

This piece is part of the Painted Conversations series, written with AI as co-author. The full essay continues on Medium.
