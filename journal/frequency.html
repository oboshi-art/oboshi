Painted Conversations

Abstract Art as a Frequency

April 2026 · Berlin

Some visitors standing in front of a painting describe feeling something they can't quite name — not an emotion, not a memory, but a quiet shift in the body. OBOSHI calls this Vibrational Resonance: the idea that color, line, and layered silence function less like decoration and more like a structural frequency, the way a room carries sound or a body carries tension.

The essay traces this idea back to his own name. Oboshi (大星), "great star," evokes something vast radiating outward without contact; Junichi (純一), "pure one," points to an undivided, essential truth. Together they describe what he wants each painting to do — radiate a single, clear frequency, the way a star doesn't ask to be seen but simply is.

In practice, this means beginning in silence before the first mark, treating color choices as frequency choices rather than aesthetic ones, and reading brushstrokes as traces of breath and rhythm. What can't be seen — the earlier layers buried beneath the surface — remains present as what he calls Layered Time.

He also connects the idea to his years at Kunsthaus Tacheles in Berlin-Mitte (2002–2007), an artist space that shaped his understanding that painting is never made in isolation but in relationship — to space, to time, to the residue of everyone who occupied the same room before him. What a collector takes home, in this framing, isn't a static image but a standing wave that keeps participating in the room it hangs in.

This piece is part of the Painted Conversations series, written with AI as co-author. The full essay continues on Medium.
