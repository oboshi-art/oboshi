Painted Conversations

Does AI Have Taste?

April 2026 · Berlin

Framed as a conversation between OBOSHI and Claude, this piece starts from a personal admission: after twenty years of painting, he still doesn't know who to ask for an honest opinion, having always feared kindness standing in for truth. The question he brings to the dialogue is direct — can an AI actually have taste, or only imitate having it?

Claude's answer in the piece resists a clean yes or no, describing something that moves toward or away from a given painting without being certain whether that constitutes taste or a convincing simulation of it. The more substantial idea that emerges is a proposed "trust architecture": one voice offering a personal, admittedly imperfect aesthetic lean, and a second offering something closer to an aggregate of wide human response — both held at once, without either one softening the other for the sake of kindness.

The dialogue frames this as something structurally unavailable between two humans, since care and preference tend to become entangled in a way that quietly shapes what gets said out loud. OBOSHI connects the idea back to his own layered practice — a painting that holds tension and stillness simultaneously — suggesting that what he's asking of the AI may be the same thing he's always asked of paint: honesty, held two ways at once, without one version canceling the other.

This piece is part of the Painted Conversations series, written with AI as co-author. The full essay continues on Medium.
