A Thought Toward One Slip After Another

May 2026 · Berlin

OBOSHI opens with the idea that a slip in a painting doesn't end where it seems to — it tilts forward by a fraction, and in that small tilt the next slip already begins. Slips, in his account, are never singular; each one carries the next inside it, without intention or decision involved.

Conscious thought about a slip always arrives late, he writes, touching only the trace of a shift that has already happened. He doesn't treat this as thinking's failure so much as its natural limit here — a slip moves faster than understanding, which may be part of why it can feel closer to truth than deliberate intention ever does.

A short dialogue with Copilot explores losing track of where the self ends amid a chain of slips, reframing that very uncertainty as what calls the next slip forward, and recasting waiting not as standing still but as letting oneself be placed just outside the slip rather than trying to follow it.

The essay closes by describing a quiet, not-yet-named position opening just beyond the chain of slips — the first appearance of what OBOSHI later calls 'the fifth position,' which the following essay picks up directly.
